# Design System: Epta Layers

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Follow One Order"**

Epta keeps a client's network running across all seven OSI layers. The site says so in two surface worlds that share one brand: the logo's seven orange-to-pink steps, one status vocabulary, one shell. Each world is a complete material system, and a page lives in exactly one.

**One request, seven stops** (main homepage `/`, `body.theme-dusk`; the lead world). An original, hand-authored flat-vector picture book at dusk. One sales order leaves the Bangalore head office, descends the seven layers as seven plain-language stops (clicked, sealed, held, guaranteed, routed, handed on, carried on cable), crosses the region to Mumbai as light, and arrives at dawn. Every scene is a real place with a real person or object: the HQ tower with its sales floor lit, a desk, a sealed envelope, two colleagues on a held call, a scooter courier, a city route map, a switch room, an engineer kneeling in a cable trench, a night view of the coast, the Mumbai branch. The order is always the same glowing card in the brand gradient, so the eye can follow it anywhere. Type is Familjen Grotesk, set big: one enormous line per stop. Grounds run from night navy through indigo and plum to an ember dawn as the story ends.

**The Site Survey** (Homepage B `/home-b`, `body.theme-b`; the daylight world). An architect's survey drawing of the client's own floor: drafting-white paper, pure-white sheets edged in ink, Barlow and Barlow Condensed like CAD annotation. The seven OSI layers are CAD layers drawn onto the floor in the brand steps, with on-plan tags, fault pins resolving to green stamps, a coverage heatmap, OEM callouts, a legend and title block, and Rev A–F captions as the engagement advances.

New pages follow the lead world unless the user directs otherwise. Each is written as a subject-specific scene inside it: a solution page as the stop that solution keeps running, a case study as its own illustrated place. Never a generic template.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Two worlds, one brand: an illustrated dusk journey (`/`) and a daylight survey drawing (`/home-b`). One world per page.
- One recurring protagonist: the order card, always in the brand gradient, with a warm glow.
- Original flat-vector scenes with one warm lamp per place, people as geometric figures in warm skin tones, no stock imagery.
- One display-scale line of type per stop; the story is read top to bottom, night to dawn.
- The seven brand steps map L7 orange to L1 pink in both worlds.
- Status colours (green, red, amber) kept apart from brand in both worlds.
- No cards around scenes, no icons in circles, no drop shadows.

## Colors

### One request, seven stops (dusk)

A night palette that warms toward dawn, with brand colour reserved for the order and the actions around it.

#### Primary
- **The order** (brand-1 to brand-7, 135deg): the order card's gradient everywhere it appears: the hero window, the spine card, every journey scene, the halt road and the LAER loop. Its glow is Order Glow (order-glow), a soft radial in the scenes and hero.
- **Brand Steps 1–7**: the order's colours and the spine's history. Passed roundels take a brand-7 ring. Brand-1 marks the "Follow one order" dot and its ping, keeper-link arrows, contact icons, and the factory route in the proof art.
- **Action** (action, hover action-hover): the burnt-orange primary button with white text (on-action). Inside illustrations, action also colours LAER flags, a checklist header and one character's shirt.
- **Dusk Accent** (dusk-accent, dusk-accent-deep): link hover and focus on night.

#### Illustration palette
- **Sky** (sky-900, sky-700, dusk-500): scene skies and the world stage's night (sky-900 to sky-700 to plum-700).
- **Structure** (ill-ground, ill-panel, ill-shadow, structure-800, structure-750, structure-725, structure-block, structure-700, road, structure-line): ground planes, the proof tile ground, deepest shadow, building faces, window frames, roads and junctions, outlines and lane dashes. Flat, cool and stepped. No gradients on structure.
- **Light** (lamp, lamp-hi): the warm key light in every scene: a radial from lamp-hi at 55% through lamp at 18% to nothing, plus streetlamp and headlamp beams. **Screen Glow** (screen-glow) is the only cool light, monitor spill. Lit windows are lamp.
- **Teal** (teal): shirts and small accents on people and equipment, never status.
- **People** (skin-light, skin-mid, skin-warm, skin-deep): warm skin tones for geometric figures. Shirts take paper-cool, teal or action; hair is near-black.
- **Props** (paper-cool, parcel, parcel-strap): paper, envelopes, checklists, parcels.
- **Junction states** (junction-fault, junction-repaired): the broken junction's amber-backed fill and its repaired green-backed fill on the halt road.
- **Per-object shades.** Individual scenes also use one-off shades inside these families rather than new hues: cool navy structure shades between #0d1534 and #2c3a6e (facades, window frames, furniture, roads), muted plum shades between #170f26 and #5a4880 (dusk buildings and interiors), near-black hair (#120f1c, #1b1b2b, #2a1b14), a darker parcel brown (#3a2410), a teal shadow (#23998b), a hard-hat ochre (#c98b3b) and a pale screen (#d6dcea). They are drawing values, not tokens. Reuse the named tokens first, and add a one-off shade only within an existing family.

#### Neutral
- **Night grounds** (hero-night, journey-night, night-900, night-800, night-750, night-700, dusk-violet, plum-700, plum-500): hero, journey, then each later section one step further into dusk. **Dawn** (dawn-violet, ember-300, horizon-gold, horizon-mauve, dawn-skyline): the journey's dawn wash, the world stage's dawn overlay, and the contact section's dawn sky and skyline.
- **Dusk Ink / Hero Lede / Ink 2 / Ink 3** (dusk-ink, hero-lede, dusk-ink-2, dusk-ink-3): active headlines and links; the hero sub-line; body; idle stop lines, meta and inactive roundels.
- **Dusk Rule / Rule Strong** (dusk-rule, dusk-rule-strong): the spine, keeper-link rules, the quote rule, roundel rings, roster slots, the form panel edge, the footer's top hairline.
- **Veils** (night-veil-panel, hero-fade): the contact form panel; the fade behind the hero headline on narrow screens.

#### Status on dusk
- **Dusk Pass** (dusk-pass): resolved and working: repaired junction ring, "ok" and "PASS" text in scenes, switch LEDs, call-live chip, campus Wi-Fi waves. **Fault** (fault): congestion, hazards and the broken junction, always amber. **Dusk Limit / Limit Ink**: form errors.

### The Site Survey (daylight)

A daylight palette of paper, rule and ink, stamped with the same seven steps.
- **Brand Steps 1–7**: the seven CAD layer strokes and their on-plan tags, the hero layer-switch swatches. Brand-1 also draws text-link underlines, the active rail step and chapter numeral, scrubber progress, live traffic dots, and focus-tint (7%) on a focused room.
- **Accent Fill** (brand-1, hover brand-3): the primary button, with ink text. **Accent** (accent, accent-deep): link hover, focus rings, caret, selection, contact icons.
- **Paper** (paper-b, sheet-white, ground, sheet, sheet-under, sheet-deep): page, drafting sheets and plan floor, and the light shell and utility pages.

**The Status-Apart Rule.** Brand colour never reports a result. Working and resolved are green, form errors are red, congestion, hazards and faults are amber. A delay or alarm is never drawn in the order's orange or pink.

**The Seven Steps Rule.** The brand steps follow the logo: brand-1 orange at L7, brand-7 pink at L1. When brand colour encodes layers, in either world, each layer gets its own step and the order is never shuffled or reversed.

**The Ink-Beside-Verdicts Rule.** On daylight pages, red-family link text beside a status reads as a failure: links are ink with a 2px brand-1 underline and turn accent only on hover.

## Elevation & Depth

**One request, seven stops.** Depth is painted, not lifted. **The Site Survey.** Flat. Paper on paper: white sheets with a 1px rule and a 6px ink top edge on drafting white. The coverage heatmap (pass green fading radially from 38%) is the only soft fill, and it is data.

### Named Rules

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** put each page wholly in one world; new pages follow the dusk journey unless the user directs otherwise.
- **Do** tell each page through a real, specific scene: a place, a person, an object the visitor recognises.
- **Do** draw the order as the same brand-gradient card with white lines in every scene, and let it glow.
- **Do** give each place in a scene one warm lamp, and build scenes from flat back, mid and front planes.
- **Do** draw people as flat geometric figures in warm skin tones, reusing the shared seated figure where it fits.
- **Do** draw congestion, hazards and faults in amber and their resolution in green.
- **Do** tell each stop with one display-scale line, a short body and the solution that keeps it running.
- **Do** use action (#c2410c) with white text for primary actions at dusk, and brand-1 with ink text by day.
- **Do** label illustrative devices as illustrative, set recorded figures inline, and leave empty partner slots visibly empty.
- **Do** letter survey-world annotation, legends, title blocks and table heads in Barlow Condensed.

### Don't:
- **Don't** mix dusk and daylight bands, sheets or type on one page.
- **Don't** put scenes in cards, frames or bordered boxes; feather the stage into the page.
- **Don't** draw national borders on maps; show regions, coasts and cities only.
- **Don't** draw a delay, alarm or fault in the order's orange or pink, or give anything but the order the full brand gradient.
- **Don't** add a second warm light to one place in a scene, or use drop shadows anywhere.
- **Don't** use roundels anywhere but the spine, or icons in circles as decoration.
- **Don't** use font glyphs or emoji as icons; draw them in SVG.
- **Don't** use stock photography, abstract tech visuals, or the headline-left/effect-right hero with repeating heading-plus-rows sections.
- **Don't** put eyebrow kickers above headings.
- **Don't** set link text in a red-family colour beside a status on a daylight page.
